After by some method the value $l_0''$ has been computed, which differs from $l_1$ by a very small quantity when regard is had to the eccentricity to the fourth degree, it is easy to compute the latitude $l_0$ more accurately.

If, expanding the longitude by Taylor's theorem, according to powers of the difference $l_0''$, we break off the series after the second term, we obtain the formula
\[
 \psi=\int_{\varphi_1}^{\varphi_2}d\varphi\,\cos l_0''{\sqrt{1-e^2+e^2\sin^2l_0''\cos^2\varphi}\over1-\sin^2l_0''\cos^2\varphi}
 +(l_0-l_0''){\partial\psi\over\partial l_0}
\]
or, putting in the values both for the integral and for ${\partial\psi\over\partial l_0}$:
\[
\text{V.}\qquad l_0=l_0''+{t_2-t_1-\psi\over u}
\]
in which formula
\[
\begin{array}{rcll}
\text{VI.}& t_2+2\varepsilon{1-2q\over1-\varepsilon}x_2\cos A+
\varepsilon q\sin2x_2\cos A&=&\arc\left(\tg={\tg x_2\over\cos A}\right) \qquad \text{cf. chap. VIII \S\ 4}\vspace{0.2em}\\
\text{VII.}& t_1+2\varepsilon{1-2q\over1-\varepsilon}x_1\cos A+
\varepsilon q\sin2x_1\cos A&=&\arc\left(\tg={\tg x_1\over\cos A}\right)\vspace{0.2em}\\
\text{VIII.}& u\sin B_0&=&8q(x_2-x_1)+(1-4q)\{\cot x_2-
\cot x_1\}\qquad \text{cf. chap. VIII \S\ 3.}
\end{array}
\]

\rule{0.35\textwidth}{0.4pt}
