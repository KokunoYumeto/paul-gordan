\begin{center}\S\ 12.\end{center}

After we have therefore understood that, with $\delta l$ suitably defined and with the geodetic line produced as far as the terminus $O$,
\[
 U=\int_{\psi_1}^{\psi_2}d\psi\{(11)\delta l'\delta l'+2(01)\delta l\delta l'+(00)\delta l\delta l\}=0
\]
can be made to hold, it remains to show that, if the line is produced farther,
\[
 U<0
\]
can exist.

Accordingly we divide our integral into two parts, so that one part is contained in the interval from $\psi_1$ to $\psi_2$, the other from $\psi_2$ to $\psi$, putting
\[
 \psi_1<\psi_2<\psi.
\]
In the first part we take
\[
 \delta l=\alpha_1{dl\over d\psi_1}+\beta_1{dl\over dl_0},
\]
and in the latter
\[
 \delta l=\alpha_2{dl\over d\psi_1}+\beta_2{dl\over dl_0},
\]
where in these formulae
\[
 \alpha_1\qquad \alpha_2\qquad \beta_1\qquad \beta_2
\]
are arbitrary constants. Hence there results:

\[
\begin{aligned}
U=\left\{\begin{aligned}
&\int_{\psi_1}^{\psi_2}d\psi\left\{(11)\left(\alpha_1{dl'\over d\psi_1}+\beta_1{dl'\over dl_0}\right)^2
+2(01)\left(\alpha_1{dl'\over d\psi_1}+\beta_1{dl'\over dl_0}\right)\left(\alpha_1{dl\over d\psi_1}+\beta_1{dl\over dl_0}\right)
+(00)\left(\alpha_1{dl\over d\psi_1}+\beta_1{dl\over dl_0}\right)^2\right\}\\

\end{aligned}\right.
\end{aligned}
\]
