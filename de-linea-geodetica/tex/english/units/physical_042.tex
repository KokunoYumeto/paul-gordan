It can happen that $l_0'$ as defined from equation II does not exhibit a value lying near $l_0$; this occurs precisely when the sum $l_1+l_2$ is very small and $\psi$ differs not far from $\pi$.

Here $\tg^2l_0$ is represented, as can easily be understood, as the quotient of very small quantities; whence it follows that even the least increment, introduced by adding the integral $\chi$ to the quantity $\psi$, produces a large change in the value of $l_0$.

If this occurs, for the purpose of computing $l_0$ one must have recourse to formula II itself; and since, by putting in place of $l_0''$ the already known value $l_0'$ which is near it, it cannot be reduced to a more elegant form, another procedure must be used, namely putting, for certain quantities, other quantities very close to them.

Since the quantity
\[
 \psi+{e^2\over2}\cos l_0''(\varphi_2-\varphi_1)
\]
in equation III is not much different from $\pi$, we may take
\[
 \sin\left[\psi+{e^2\over2}\cos l_0''(\varphi_2-\varphi_1)\right]
=\pi-\psi-{e^2\over2}\cos l_0''(\varphi_2-\varphi_1)
\]
\[
 \cos\left[\psi+{e^2\over2}\cos l_0''(\varphi_2-\varphi_1)\right]
=-1+{1\over2}\left\{\pi-\psi-{e^2\over2}\cos l_0''(\varphi_2-\varphi_1)\right\}^2.
\]
Thus
\[
\begin{aligned}
\tg^2l_0''\left\{\pi-\psi-{e^2\over2}\cos l_0''(\varphi_2-\varphi_1)\right\}^2
&=\tg^2l_1+\tg^2l_2+2\tg l_1\tg l_2\\
&\quad-\tg l_1\tg l_2\left\{\pi-\psi-{e^2\over2}\cos l_0''(\varphi_2-\varphi_1)\right\}^2
\end{aligned}
\]
\[
\left\{\tg^2l_0''+\tg l_1\tg l_2\right\}\left\{\pi-\psi-{e^2\over2}\cos l_0''(\varphi_2-\varphi_1)\right\}^2
=\left\{\tg l_1+\tg l_2\right\}^2.
\]

\cos\varphi_2$ is very small, and hence either $\varphi_2+\varphi_1$ or $\varphi_2-\varphi_1$ does not recede far from $\pi$. Since this cannot fall in the sum, because the simple angle $\varphi_2$ is greater than $\pi$ (for we have put the longitude $\psi$ as lying near $\pi$, and so
\[

\qquad \text{cf. p. 35}
\]
holds), the difference \gq{$\varphi_2-\varphi_1$} lies near $\pi$, so that in place of $\varphi_2-\varphi_1$ one may put $\pi$.
